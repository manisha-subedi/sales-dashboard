Sales performance dashboard

This project uses a practice sales dataset for a technology company. It has 10,000 order lines, 4,596 orders, and 795 customers across 15 European countries from January 2020 to December 2023.

I used the data to build a two-page Tableau dashboard. It compares sales, profit, orders, and customers with the previous year. This page shows the same results and looks more closely at the change in sales.

The Tableau dashboard

The executive summary compares four main measures with the previous year. It also shows monthly results, segment and category comparisons, and a country map. The customer page shows new and returning customers, the top customers, and profit by sub-category.

What changed sales?

Sales grew every year, but the number of customers changed very little. The chart groups the change into new customers, customers who came back after a year away, returning customers who spent more or less, and customers who did not order.

How I built this

The source is one CSV with 10,000 rows and a second small file that maps 127 states and regions to their countries. DuckDB SQL turns them into a small star schema: one fact table with an order line per row, and dimensions for customers, places, and dates.

The source needed a few checks. Dates use day-month-year format. Two manual year markers were removed because the year can be taken from the order date. Some order IDs appear with more than one customer or date, so orders are counted as distinct IDs, as they were in the original dashboard.

One table stores sales per customer and year. Customers are placed in five groups by comparing their sales with the previous year. A test checks that the five groups add up to the total change.

The build writes JSON files that this page reads, and one flat CSV that the Tableau workbook connects to. The code, SQL, tests, and the Tableau guide are on GitHub.
