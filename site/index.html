Four years of sales at a tech company

The data is 10,000 order lines from a practice dataset about a tech company that sells hardware, software, and services in 15 European countries, from January 2020 to December 2023. It has 4,596 orders and 795 customers.

I built a Tableau dashboard on it for an executive audience: sales, profit, orders, and customers against the year before, by segment, category, and country. This page shows the same numbers as plain charts, and adds a breakdown of where each year's growth came from.

The Tableau dashboard

Two pages. The executive summary has the four KPIs with the previous year as a mark on every month, a year and metric picker, segment and category comparisons with small trend lines, and a country map. Click a country to filter the page. The customers page shows new and returning customers, the top customers, and profit by sub-category.

Where did the growth come from?

Sales grew every year, but the customer count barely moved. This splits each year's change in sales by who caused it: new customers, customers who came back after a year away, returning customers who spent more or less than the year before, and customers who did not order at all.

How I built this

The source is one CSV with 10,000 rows and a second small file that maps 127 states and regions to their countries. DuckDB SQL turns them into a small star schema: one fact table with an order line per row, and dimensions for customers, places, and dates.

Three things in the source needed care. Dates are written day first, so they are parsed that way. The file has two columns that mark the current and last year by hand, and they are dropped, the year comes from the order date instead. And some order ids appear with more than one customer or date, so orders are counted as distinct ids, the same way the original dashboard did.

The growth split works on one table with sales per customer per year. For each year, every customer is put in one of five groups by comparing their sales with the year before. A test checks that the five groups add up to the total change.

The build writes JSON files that this page reads, and one flat CSV that the Tableau workbook connects to. The code, SQL, tests, and the Tableau guide are on GitHub.
